🧰 Tool capabilities
Opportunity Pipeline is a zero-backend, browser-based contract intelligence dashboard that processes the full SAM.gov Contract Opportunities dataset. It requires no API keys, no server, and no subscriptions.

Data ingestion: Upload the SAM.gov full CSV (150K+ records). Auto-filters to your NAICS codes. Deduplicates on Notice ID across multiple uploads.

Three views: Opportunities (open solicitations with scoring), Sub-Contracting Leads (awarded contracts with awardee intelligence), and Insights (trend analysis, BCG matrix, recruitment recommendations).

Configurable from the UI: NAICS codes, company certifications, and capability keywords — all stored in browser localStorage. No code changes needed.

Technology: Pure HTML + CSS + JavaScript + Chart.js. No frameworks, no build step, no AI API. Deployable on Netlify (free) via drag-and-drop.
⚙️ Algorithms
Match % — opportunity scoring engine
Each opportunity is scored on a 0–100% scale using four rule-based components. No AI or machine learning is used — all scoring is deterministic and transparent.

Component 1 — NAICS code match (0–40 pts)
Exact match to a configured code = 40 pts. Prefix match (first 4 digits) = 12 pts. No match = 0 pts.

Component 2 — Capability keyword match (0–35 pts)
Contract description is scanned for phrases extracted from your company's service descriptions (pasted in the NAICS drawer). Each keyword hit adds points, capped at 35. More hits = higher relevance.

Component 3 — Set-aside certification match (−25 to +15 pts)
If the contract's set-aside requires a certification you hold (configured in drawer) = +15 pts. If it requires a cert you don't hold (SDVOSB, 8(a), HUBZone, etc.) = −25 pts. Generic set-aside = +5 pts.

Component 4 — Clearance penalty (0 to −10 pts)
Clearance Required = −10 pts. Citizenship Required = −5 pts. Open = 0 pts.
📊 KPI interpretation guide
75–100%
Strong match. NAICS code is exact, description mentions your capabilities, and set-aside aligns with your certifications. Prioritize these for bid review.
50–74%
Moderate match. Likely a NAICS match with some keyword hits. Worth reviewing. May need additional teaming partner or capability to strengthen the bid.
25–49%
Partial match. NAICS prefix match or keyword-only relevance. Review if capacity allows. Good candidate for subcontracting rather than prime pursuit.
0–24%
Weak match. Limited alignment. May appear due to set-aside restriction (−25 pts) on an otherwise relevant NAICS code. Check if the set-aside column shows red — if so, the contract requires a certification you don't hold.
0%
No match. No NAICS alignment, no keyword hits, and possibly a restricted set-aside. Skip unless pursuing as a subcontractor under another prime.

⚖ Understanding FAR 19.702 — Your biggest subcontracting advantage
What it is: Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) Subpart 19.7, Section 702. A federal law requiring any prime contractor that wins a government contract over $900,000 to submit a formal subcontracting plan and make documented good-faith efforts to award subcontract work to small businesses, minority-owned businesses (MBE), women-owned businesses, veteran-owned businesses, and HUBZone firms.

Why it matters: Primes that miss their subcontracting targets face consequences — poor past performance ratings, which directly hurt their ability to win future contracts. This creates genuine urgency for them to find and use MBE/SB subcontractors.

Your leverage: As an MBE + Small Business, you satisfy two of the government's priority categories simultaneously. When approaching a prime with a contract over $900K, your pitch is not "please hire us" — it's "we help you meet your legal compliance obligation and protect your past performance score." That is a fundamentally different and stronger conversation.

In this tool: Every awardee profile shows a "FAR 19.702" badge when their total contract value exceeds $900K. The export CSV includes a "FAR 19.702 Eligible" column sorted to the top — hand this directly to your CEO for outreach prioritization.
